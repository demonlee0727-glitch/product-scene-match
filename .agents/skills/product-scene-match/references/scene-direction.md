# Scene direction

## Read the camera cues

| Inspect | What to observe | What to change in the scene |
|---|---|---|
| Rim, opening and top face | Amount of visible top surface, visible side and apparent ellipse. Combine multiple cues before judging camera height. | Adjust support depth, vanishing directions and camera tilt. One photograph rarely determines an exact focal length. |
| Vertical axis and base | Product rotation, elevation and actual point of contact | Set the horizon and wall junctions. Extend the support beneath the entire footprint. |
| Material and highlights | Light direction, size, softness and color | Match the scene lighting and ground shadow. Preserve the product's original color and highlights. |
| Scene identity | Two or three defining elements such as stone, wall corners or architectural fins | Keep them recognizable as the geometry changes. Compare front and rear edges and the wall junction. |
| Elements to clear | Old product, fragments, reflections, cast shadows, letters and digits | Restore the correct texture on each plane. Look for reflected writing, labels and artificial letter-like marks. |
| Openings and transparency | Handle holes, translucent material and residual color from the old background | Preserve alpha and inspect the new background through openings. Glass with baked-in refraction may need a prepared cutout or original render passes. |

## Brief for an empty plate

Replace each bracket with an observation from the actual inputs. Send the reference to an editor that supports image-conditioned editing.

> Rebuild the same [space] shown in Image A. Preserve [materials and colors], [key props and positions] and [main light direction]. Remove the product from [location], including its shadow and reflections. Clear all lettering from [specific areas] and reconstruct the material behind it. Image B is a camera and lighting reference only. Its visible [top and side features] require [specific support-plane and wall-edge changes]. Leave a complete support surface at [contact location]. Produce an empty [aspect ratio] background at the target resolution where possible. Leave the plate free of products, substitutes, placeholder models, letters and numbers.

If the reference lighting conflicts with the fixed product photo, adjust window light and fill in the scene. Crop or extend only the background to correct its aspect ratio. Record the native plate dimensions. Where a major viewpoint change reveals hidden areas, describe those parts of the environment as reconstructed.

## Common corrections

- **Letter-shaped patches after cleanup.** Repair texture or reflections locally, then inspect all affected regions again.
- **A believable plinth with skewed vertical fins.** Rebuild the wall plane and check the shared edges and vanishing directions.
- **A changed cup rim, handle or bottle silhouette.** Discard the redrawn foreground and composite the original product layer.
- **A floating base or detached shadow.** Locate the true contact point, revise the scene shadow, then place the original foreground at the same coordinates.
- **Old surroundings inside tinted glass.** Use a clean transparency pass or original render layers when available. A flattened photograph may not contain enough information to separate the refraction accurately.
